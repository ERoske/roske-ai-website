I pay $200 a month for the top Claude plan, and most weeks I'm about to run out of it right before it resets on Thursday at 2PM. I admitted that on my own show a couple of months ago (to Elsa Del Pino, who was already on usage credits that week, which made me feel better for about 4 seconds), and she knew the reset time down to the day before I said the hour out loud, which tells you we'd both been watching that clock. The plan doesn't say "runs out Thursday" anywhere on the price, it just says Max (which, in fairness, is exactly how I use it). [Anthropic added weekly limits](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/28/anthropic-unveils-new-rate-limits-to-curb-claude-code-power-users/) to its paid plans back in 2025 to rein in the people running its coding tool around the clock, and if you go over, you can buy more at the regular API rates. (Edward, a man who has written 15 books about software is now admitting in public that he can't ration a chatbot for 7 days, and he put it in the first paragraph, where even the people who only read first paragraphs will see it.)

For the record, I'm a happy customer (happy enough to keep running out of it, anyway).

It's Black Friday, so [my website](/) spent last night camped out in a store parking lot waiting for an AI doorbuster (in pictures, anyway, with a thermos, a plaid blanket, a plate of leftovers, 4 orange traffic cones holding up a rope, and a magnifying glass for later), and this morning the home page is a sale circular. Every item on it has a big price with a slashed "compare at" price next to it, and then an asterisk, and the asterisks took a lot longer to write than the prices did. The machine that builds the site normally shows up at 5AM on Saturdays, so this is the first week I've ever asked it to work the overnight shift on a holiday… it didn't complain, which I'm choosing to take as enthusiasm.

The fine print is where I'd spend this weekend (it's usually on display in the bottom of a locked filing cabinet stuck in a disused lavatory with a sign on the door saying "Beware of the Leopard," and the Vogons at least had the decency to put their plans on display somewhere). AI pricing has been moving all year, and most of the movement happens below the banner. Salesforce launched its Agentforce agents at $2 per conversation and then [added Flex Credits](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/flex-credits/), which charge 10 cents an action instead, and the credits you don't use don't roll over into the next term. Microsoft put Copilot into the consumer versions of Microsoft 365 and [raised the price $3 a month at renewal](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/microsoft-office/microsoft-365-sees-43-percent-price-hike-thanks-to-copilot-existing-customers-safe-until-renewal), which on the Personal plan works out to about 43% (they did offer a Classic plan for people who didn't want the AI, which you'd only know about if you went looking for it). I'm not saying either of those is a bad deal, and plenty of people will get their money's worth out of both.

Here's my conflict of interest, since it's a holiday and I'm feeling honest. I spent 25 years running interRel, a company that lived on the part of the receipt that wasn't the software. Hyperion (and later Oracle) sold the license, and then somebody had to connect it to the customer's real data, clean that data up first, build the thing, and spend months teaching a few hundred people why they couldn't keep their own spreadsheet on the side anymore, and that somebody was us. If the license had been the expensive part, there wouldn't have been an interRel. AI looks like the same deal to me with a new banner over the door (and the models keep getting cheaper every few months, which I'm told is not true of consultants).

None of this means I'd wait. On my show I said, "If I see one more organization trying to use a free model, it just drives me insane," and I meant it, because the free version is the worst deal in the whole store. You pay with your data, and the model you're helping train gets sold to the company down the street. Start with $20 a month and see what your people actually do with it. And if somebody offers you a free pilot, take it, but put a meeting on the calendar for day 85 where somebody has to decide what happens on day 91, and make it somebody who'll actually show up to the meeting.

If you're sitting on an AI order form that expires at the end of a quarter, send me the fine print (Edward@Roske.AI) and I'll read it with you, and I won't even try to sell you anything. Happy Black Friday!
